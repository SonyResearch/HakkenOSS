FOXO3 (Forkhead box O3) is a member of the forkhead box O family of transcription factors and a key downstream effector of insulin/IGF-1 and PI3K–AKT signaling, where it regulates gene programs involved in oxidative stress resistance, autophagy, apoptosis, and metabolic homeostasis. FOXO3 has been repeatedly implicated in human longevity: genetic association studies across multiple populations have identified specific FOXO3 single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) that are enriched among long-lived individuals, including centenarians, with reported odds ratios commonly in the range of ~1.2–2.0 for exceptional longevity depending on cohort and allele. Functionally, reduced insulin/IGF-1 signaling promotes FOXO3 nuclear localization and transcriptional activation, a conserved mechanism that extends lifespan in model organisms; for example, attenuation of IIS/AKT-mediated FOXO inhibition is associated with lifespan increases on the order of ~20–100% in canonical invertebrate aging models. Collectively, these findings position FOXO3 as a central integrator of nutrient-sensing pathways and cellular maintenance processes that influence aging trajectories and lifespan variability.
