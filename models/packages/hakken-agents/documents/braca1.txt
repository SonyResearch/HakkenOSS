BRCA1 plays a central role in the cellular DNA damage response, particularly in the repair of DNA double-strand breaks. Upon genotoxic stress, BRCA1 is recruited to sites of DNA damage where it functions as a scaffold protein, coordinating the assembly of multiprotein repair complexes. It facilitates homologous recombination by regulating DNA end resection and promoting the recruitment of key repair factors, thereby enabling high-fidelity repair using a sister chromatid template. Through these mechanisms, BRCA1 contributes to the maintenance of genomic integrity and suppresses the accumulation of oncogenic mutations.
